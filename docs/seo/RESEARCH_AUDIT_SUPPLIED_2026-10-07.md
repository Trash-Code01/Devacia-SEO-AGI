# Devacia research audit
Audit date: 7 October 2026. Research and recommendations only; no site, analytics, campaign or outreach changes made.

## Verdict
The report is a useful strategic draft, but is not ready to serve as an implementation specification. Preserve outcome-led industry pages, evidence-led case studies, descriptive internal links and CRM reconciliation. Correct the analytics interpretation, unverified results, demand rankings, scope restrictions and technical claims before implementation.

Public sources were opened on the audit date; retrieval can contain cached text and is not a deployment or source-code inspection. Keyword Tool was checked for Google / India / English. Its autocomplete suggestions are candidate discovery, not rankings or proof of purchase intent.

Missing primary inputs: the 200-keyword workbook, original research, GROWTH_EXECUTION_2026-10-07.md, ga4-growth-baseline-2026-10-07.json and INDUSTRY_SEO_HANDOVER_2026-10-07.md. This is a proof-and-readiness judgement, not proof that these industries have the highest search demand or closing rate. Preserve completed, sound work in other industries rather than discarding it to fit a new sequence.

## Compliance with the original brief
- Twelve page outlines and a broad priority matrix are present. Useful foundation.
- Only seven keyword rows are provided, not meaningful coverage across twelve industries; comparison, cost and workflow clusters are incomplete. Count alone is not the issue: decision coverage and evidence are.
- Four search-evidence rows are weakly reproducible. “Oct 2026” is not an exact observation date. The restaurant row cites a Devacia Sortlist profile, which cannot substantiate a claim about most restaurant agencies. The wholesale row lacks exact result URLs.
- Calling the tables CSV is imprecise: they are text tables. The brief allowed exportable tables, so lack of separate downloads alone is not a failure.
- Several page briefs lack researched FAQs, cost/ownership answers, concise opening copy and exact internal-link mapping.
- Canonical handover use, existing keyword IDs and local implementation reconciliation are not demonstrated.
- The backlog has six broad tasks, not a dependency-complete 90-day plan.
- Preserve the report's rejection of fabricated offices, doorway expansion, special AI markup and unverifiable keyword volumes. Its errors do not invalidate these sound recommendations.

## Unresolved assumptions behind 20×
First define the outcome, attribution rule and comparable cohort/window. A useful model is:

Qualified enquiries = relevant sessions × enquiry rate × acceptance rate × qualification rate.
Won projects = qualified enquiries × close rate.
Contracted value = won projects × average signed project value.
Collected revenue depends on payment timing, collection rate, refunds/cancellations and the cohort being measured.

If enquiry rate is already defined using accepted enquiries, omit acceptance rate rather than counting it twice.

Blocking unknowns:
1. Current attributable accepted enquiries, qualified enquiries, wins and collected revenue; an unknown or zero denominator does not support a meaningful growth multiple.
2. The meaning and actual trigger of the single qualified_lead event.
3. Country, industry, branded/non-branded and landing-page mix of the small traffic baseline.
4. Enquiry, acceptance, qualification and closing rates with denominators and sales-cycle lag.
5. Actual average contract value, payment schedule, refund exposure and recurring revenue treatment.
6. Sales response capacity, fulfilment capacity and contribution margin at greater volume.
7. Which industry pages are already implemented and what the approved starting scope includes.
8. Valid buyer demand and achievable visibility in the target markets.

The report's 19.95× illustration assumes close rate, acceptance (if separate) and collection behaviour do not worsen. It also requires feasible rates: doubling a 60% enquiry rate would be impossible. None of its chosen multipliers has been empirically established for Devacia. Use absolute targets alongside growth multiples until a reliable baseline exists.
